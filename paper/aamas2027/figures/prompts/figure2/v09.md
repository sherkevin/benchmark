## Prompt to copy

Create a compact, publication-ready flat-vector timing diagram for a top multi-agent systems paper. Use a 1600×560 white canvas and fill most of the canvas: margins about 40–55 px, occupied width at least 85%, and only a 35–55 px gap between the upper action band and lower feedback band. Keep the elegant no-grid event-spine style from v08, but make the composition dense and connected rather than airy. This is a research architecture figure, not a dashboard, table, checklist, or infographic.

Use only two close visual bands and five small stage pills above the upper band: “decision t”, “selected call”, “terminal”, “pending D”, and “release / next action”. Do not draw full-width horizontal rules, vertical column lines, lane separators, cell borders, or a regular grid. Keep cards close enough that every arrow reads as one continuous process.

Upper action band, left to right, in one straight chain: light-grey “S_t + A_t” → navy “selector” → navy “one selected provider” → light-grey “call” → amber “terminal record” with small badges “output” and “runtime status” → amber “frozen endpoint judge” → small amber “chemical correctness”. Keep terminal record, judge, and chemical correctness on the same horizontal baseline; the judge must not look like a side module.

Immediately below the chemical-correctness badge, place a compact amber chip “terminal outcome”. Draw a short downward arrow from chemical correctness to terminal outcome. Draw a short separate grey branch from the terminal record’s runtime status to “system failure”, then a short arrow from “system failure” into the same terminal-outcome chip. The two branches meet only at terminal outcome.

The lower feedback band must be one unbroken left-to-right chain beginning directly below terminal outcome: terminal outcome → selected outcome → selected-only queue → released outcome → relay update → S_τt → first action at or after τ_t. Use a short vertical arrow from terminal outcome to selected outcome. Put the selected-only queue physically between selected outcome and released outcome, never beside or after released outcome. No arrow may skip selected outcome or the queue; no arrow from terminal outcome may point directly to released outcome. Label the purple queue “selected-only queue” and “τ_t = t + 1 + D”. Use one purple dashed waiting segment through the queue. Above that segment, place two or three small grey dots labelled “t+1 … τ_t−1” and one short note “event hidden until τ_t”.

After the queue, show navy “released outcome” → compact navy “relay update” with the short sublabel “family residual + provider bias” → navy state chip “S_τt” → light-grey “first action at or after τ_t”. Add a thin navy return arrow from S_τt toward the future selector, with a short label “future selector reads released state”; the arrow must land after release and never enter the current action. Under S_τt add only “D=0: visible before next action”.

Keep only one tiny pale-grey provider card near the left margin with a closed-eye icon and the label “unselected output not observed”. Do not draw a fallback or diagnostic-ledger card in this figure; Figure 1 carries that bookkeeping path. Do not use a large crossed-out box.

Use navy #2E5DA1 for selected actions, selected outcome, relay update, released state, and completed return flow; light grey #B8C0CC for state, call, system failure, and unavailable observations; amber #D99000 for terminal record, frozen judge, chemical correctness, and terminal outcome; muted purple #6B4C9A for the pending queue and waiting segment; black or charcoal text. Use solid arrows for completed flow, purple dashed arrows for waiting, and one thin blue return arrow for future-state reuse. Use one clean Arial/Helvetica/Inter sans-serif font, medium outlines, subtle corner rounding, no gradients, no 3-D, no drop shadows, and no decorative characters.

Keep visible labels short and exact. Do not add equations beyond “τ_t = t + 1 + D”, probabilities, metrics, provider names, long subtitles, numbered steps, full paragraphs, extra agents, refusal, escalation, verifier, immediate feedback, online training loops, fake values, or a circular same-action loop. Do not imply that positive delay means “release → decision t+1”.
