## Observed Patterns

1. Both RE families show a decrease in Tc with increasing Pr content.
2. The Er-family maintains higher Tc values at higher Pr concentrations compared to the Nd-family.
   - At Pr=0.030: Er-family Tc = 93.9K vs Nd-family Tc = 85.1K
   - At Pr=0.076-0.080: Er-family Tc = 91.1K vs Nd-family Tc = 68.2K
   - At Pr=0.150: Er-family Tc = 82.9K vs Nd-family Tc = 40.7K
   - At Pr=0.200: Er-family Tc = 76.4K vs Nd-family Tc = 31.4K
   - At Pr=0.300: Er-family Tc = 61.1K vs Nd-family Tc = 5.6K
   - At Pr=0.400: Er-family Tc = 45.8K vs Nd-family Tc ≈ 0.0K (interpolated)
   - At Pr=0.500: Er-family Tc = 28.6K vs Nd-family Tc = 0.0K
3. The Nd-family shows a steeper decline in Tc with Pr doping compared to the Er-family.
4. Both families reach Tc = 0.0K at high Pr concentrations (Nd-family reaches 0.0K at Pr≥0.500 vs Er-family reaching 0.0K at Pr≥0.800).

## Reasoning for Gd-family Predictions

Based on the observed patterns, the Gd-family should exhibit Tc values between those of the Er-family (higher Tc) and Nd-family (lower Tc) at corresponding Pr concentrations, since Gd is between Nd and Er in the lanthanide series and likely has intermediate properties.

The Tc suppression due to Pr doping follows a similar trend for all RE elements but with varying sensitivity. Gd is positioned between Nd and Er in the lanthanide series, so I expect the Gd-family to show intermediate behavior between the two measured families. Since Gd is closer to Er in terms of ionic radius and electronic properties than it is to Nd, the Gd-family will likely be closer to the Er-family curve but with slightly lower Tc values.
