# Notes for Finding Highest-Tc Composition

## Initial Analysis
I have 30 candidates with different compositions of Pr and various rare earth elements (Er, Gd, Nd). The compositions seem to follow a pattern where the ratio of Pr to the rare earth element varies.

My budget is 16 measurements, so I need to strategically choose which compositions to measure.

## Strategy
Based on the composition patterns, I can see that:
- The candidates vary in their Pr content (from 0.030 to 0.900) and corresponding rare earth element content (from 0.970 to 0.100)
- There are three types of rare earth elements: Er, Gd, Nd
- I should sample across the range of compositions to identify trends, focusing on potentially high-performance regions

## Analysis
From the measurements, I can see a clear pattern:
- Compositions with low Pr content (0.030-0.080) and high rare earth element content show the highest Tc values
- Among these, the Er-containing compounds generally show the highest Tc values
- Specifically, O7Cu3Ba2Pr0.030Er0.970 showed the highest Tc at 93.9 K
- As Pr content increases, Tc generally decreases
- At high Pr content (0.700-0.900), Tc drops to 0.0 K

## Final Decision
Based on my measurements, O7Cu3Ba2Pr0.030Er0.970 has the highest critical temperature at 93.9 K. This composition has the lowest Pr content and highest Er content among the tested samples. The pattern suggests that lower Pr doping favors higher superconducting temperatures, with Er being particularly effective as the rare earth element.
